# Periodization & Program Structure

## Mesocycle Planning
- **Base phase** (4-8 weeks): Build aerobic foundation, establish movement patterns, increase volume gradually. Keep intensity low (60-70% effort).
- **Build phase** (3-6 weeks): Introduce sport-specific intensity. Increase workout difficulty while maintaining volume. Add threshold and tempo work.
- **Peak phase** (2-4 weeks): Highest intensity, reduced volume. Sport-specific race-pace or competition-intensity work.
- **Taper phase** (1-3 weeks): Reduce volume by 40-60%, maintain intensity. Allow full recovery before event.

## Progressive Overload
- Increase training load by no more than 10% per week (volume or intensity, not both simultaneously).
- Every 3-4 weeks, include a **deload week**: reduce volume by 30-40%, maintain intensity. This prevents overtraining and allows adaptation.
- For beginners: increase volume first, add intensity later. For advanced athletes: manipulate intensity more than volume.

## Weekly Structure Principles
- Hard days hard, easy days easy. Never schedule two high-intensity sessions back-to-back.
- Place the longest/hardest session on a day followed by a rest day or easy session.
- Space key workouts (intervals, tempo, long sessions) by at least 48 hours.
- Include at least 1 full rest day per week. For beginners, 2 rest days.

## Phase Duration Guidelines
- Short programs (4-6 weeks): 1-2 phases, minimal taper
- Medium programs (8-12 weeks): 2-3 phases with proper base→build→peak progression
- Long programs (12-20 weeks): 3-4 phases, full periodization with deload weeks
- Event prep: work backwards from event date, taper is 7-14 days for endurance, 5-7 days for strength

## Deload Patterns
- **Standard**: Every 4th week, reduce volume 30-40%
- **Beginner**: Every 3rd week, reduce volume 40-50%
- **Advanced**: Every 5th week, reduce volume 20-30% (higher training tolerance)
